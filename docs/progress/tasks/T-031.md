# T-031 — Stop the device-flow store sweeping its whole map on every request

The pending-authorization store drops expired entries by walking its entire map, and it does that on both `start` and `poll`. The map is bounded at ten thousand entries, so a full one costs ten thousand iterations per request, and filling it costs on the order of fifty million.

Both endpoints are unauthenticated by design, and `start` is what creates entries. So an attacker can fill the map with entries only they can redeem and then make every subsequent request, theirs and everyone's, do ten thousand units of pointless work. It is not a dramatic amplification, but it is unbounded work driven by an anonymous caller on the one endpoint that must stay open, and it grows with exactly the thing an attacker controls.

There is a second cost, already paid: the test that fills the store to its bound takes nine seconds under load and was one of the two tests that pushed the whole suite past its timeout. The test is honest; the quadratic cost is the production code's.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Expiry no longer costs a full scan per request. An ordered structure keyed on expiry, or a periodic sweep on a timer rather than on the request path, are both reasonable; say which and why.
- [ ] The bound still holds, and a full store still refuses new authorizations rather than growing.
- [ ] Expired entries are still unredeemable the instant they expire, whatever the sweep schedule. Correctness must not depend on a sweep having run.
- [ ] The bound test gets meaningfully faster, and says what it costs now.
- [ ] Behaviour under the bound is unchanged, including the codes returned.

## Notes

Found by T-029 while measuring why a test was slow, and correctly identified as a property of the production sweep rather than the test.

Note this store is in-memory and per-process, which is a separate limitation worth thinking about while you are here: with more than one server instance, a device authorization started on one and polled on another simply fails. That is not this task, but say whether your change makes it harder or easier to move the store into the database later.
